# Design: Bento Grid + Glassmorphism, Yale Blue

Screenshots: `output/screenshots/problem10/`

| What I changed | Why it helps customers stay and buy |
|---|---|
| **Yale Blue everywhere.** `#00356B` with Yale's secondary blues across the header, hero, buttons, prices and footer, over a soft blue background. | Shoppers instantly see "official Yale," which builds trust before they spend. One accent color also makes every button and price easy to spot. |
| **Bento home page.** Tiles of different sizes: hero, "1975" stat, a "Shop favorite" product, the assistant, 5 category tiles, "Rep your corner", and "Visit the shop". | The whole store fits on one screen, so there's no long scroll to find a starting point. Every tile is a door into the shop: a category, a product, the chat or a college/team search. |
| **Real product photos in the tiles.** Each category tile shows an actual item from the catalogue with its price. | People buy what they can see. Photos on the home page lead straight to product pages instead of text links. |
| **Strong glassmorphism.** Big Yale-blue light shapes drift slowly behind the page, and the panels are mostly see-through (about 30% white) with a heavy 26px blur, bright edges and a light sheen: header, tiles, cards, toolbar, product info, chat. Text that used to sit straight on the background got its own frosted panel. | Feels modern and premium, which supports $68–$98 prices. The moving color behind the frosted glass gives the site depth, so it feels like a real brand rather than a template. Text stays readable on every panel. |
| **Clear hierarchy.** Big blue gradient page titles, slab-serif brand type, and prices in blue pills. | Shoppers find the name and price at a glance, so comparing items and deciding takes less effort. |
| **Photo frames match each photo.** The frame takes the photo's own background color, rounded and with a hover zoom. | The 102 photos come with mixed black and white backgrounds; matched frames make the grid look clean and intentional instead of patchy. Zoom invites a closer look. |
| **Motion.** Tiles and cards rise in one after another, cards lift on hover, and the chat springs open. All of it is turned off when "reduce motion" is on. | Small movement draws the eye to products and signals that cards are clickable, without slowing anyone down. |
| **Chat that feels part of the store.** A glass chat panel with a blue header, blue shopper messages, and product cards inside replies; the home page has an "Ask the shop assistant" tile. | The assistant is easy to find and looks like the shop's own help, not an add-on. That gets more questions answered (sizes, stock, similar items) and fewer shoppers leaving unsure. |
| **Works on phones.** The bento grid goes from 4 columns to 2 to 1, with no sideways scrolling at 390px. | Most students shop on their phones, and a layout that breaks loses the sale. |
